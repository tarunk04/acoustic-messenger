Speaker-to-microphone messaging in the 19–22 kHz band. Each symbol carries up to 128 parallel tones (32 groups × 2 bits, an “audio QR”), synced by an up/down chirp that also corrects clock and Doppler drift, protected by Reed–Solomon (≈50% parity) and CRC, with acknowledged delivery. No server or network carries the messages.

If devices do not see each other

  • Both devices must use identical band and mode. Open the app on the second device by scanning the QR code above; it carries the settings.
  • Run Self-test on each device first. If it fails, that phone cannot play or hear its own 19–22 kHz tones: raise media volume to maximum, try Long range, or try the Audible band to confirm everything else works.
  • Use Tone sweep to see which tones your hardware passes. Many phone speakers and microphones roll off above 20–21 kHz; weak tones are compensated, but dead ones cost error-correction budget.
  • Max bandwidth carries the most data per second; Fast turnaround and Turbo have shorter symbols that tolerate hand movement better but resist echoes less; Long range concentrates power into few tones for distance.
  • Many simultaneous tones need about 7 dB more signal than a single tone. If messages fail beyond 1 m, move closer or switch to Long range.
  • Loud multi-tone playback can produce a faint audible buzz on some speakers (intermodulation). Lower the volume a little if you hear it. Some young people can hear 19 kHz; use 20–22 kHz to avoid that.
  • Disconnect Bluetooth audio, keep the microphone uncovered, and keep the screen on (mobile browsers pause background audio).
